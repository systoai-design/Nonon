## Overview

This lesson explains how plants convert light energy into stored sugar using chloroplasts, identifies the three necessary inputs, and details the two stages of the process.

## Plan

| Time | Section | What happens | Teacher notes | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0-10 min | **Introduction to Photosynthesis** | Students read the definition of photosynthesis and identify the location where it occurs inside the plant. | Explain that the process happens primarily inside chloroplasts within leaf cells. | photosynthesis.txt:3 |
| 10-18 min | **The Role of Chlorophyll** | Students discuss how the green pigment absorbs specific light wavelengths to drive the process. | Clarify that chlorophyll absorbs red and blue light while reflecting green light. | photosynthesis.txt:6 |
| 18-28 min | **Inputs and Transport Systems** | Students list the three required inputs and trace how water and carbon dioxide enter the plant. | Highlight that water travels up the xylem and carbon dioxide enters through stomata. | photosynthesis.txt:9 |
| 28-38 min | **Stage One: Light Reactions** | Students map the location of light reactions in thylakoid membranes and the products created. | Describe how water is split to create ATP and NADPH. | photosynthesis.txt:14 |
| 38-45 min | **Stage Two: The Calvin Cycle** | Students explain how the Calvin cycle uses previous molecules to produce sugar in the stroma. | Emphasize that this stage occurs in the stroma and results in sugar production. | photosynthesis.txt:14 |

## Check for understanding

Students will write a brief summary identifying the three inputs, the two stages, and the final product of photosynthesis.
